**Patrick Winston’s MIT presentation framework** is a structured, psychology‑backed system for crafting clear, memorable, high‑impact talks. It comes from his legendary MIT lecture *How to Speak*, delivered annually for 40+ years. The framework has two major components:

- **The Empowerment Promise** — the core rule for how to *start* a talk  
- **Winston’s Star** — the 5‑element structure for making ideas unforgettable  

Below is a complete, citation‑grounded explanation.

---

## 🎯 Core Idea (Takeaway)
Patrick Winston’s framework teaches that **every minute of your talk must earn the next minute**, and the entire presentation must deliver on a single, specific *Empowerment Promise*.   [Github](https://github.com/machinarii/awesome-mental-models/blob/master/mental-models/patrick-winstons-mit-communication-framework.md)

---

## 🧩 Part 1 — The Empowerment Promise (How to Start)
Winston insisted that **every talk must open with one sentence**:

> *“By the end of this talk, you will be able to ___.”*

This is the **Empowerment Promise**, and it must be:
- **Specific** (not “learn about X” but “be able to do Y”)  
- **Outcome‑driven**  
- **Impossible for the audience to ignore**  
- **Deliverable in the allotted time**  

He also banned weak openings:
- No jokes  
- No “thank you for having me”  
- No apologies  

These rules appear consistently in Winston’s own lecture and in documented summaries of his framework.   [Github](https://github.com/machinarii/awesome-mental-models/blob/master/mental-models/patrick-winstons-mit-communication-framework.md)

## ⭐ Part 2 — Winston’s Star (The 5 Elements of a Memorable Talk)
Winston’s Star is the heart of the framework. It states that every unforgettable presentation contains **five elements**:  




### 1. **Symbol**  
A visual or physical object that instantly represents your idea.  
- Helps the audience recall the message later.  
  [Github](https://www.choicehacking.com/2024/03/21/use-this-framework-for-fascinating-memorable-presentations-backed-by-science/)

### 2. **Slogan**  
A short, repeatable phrase that captures the core idea.  
- Should be something the audience can repeat tomorrow without explanation.  
  [Github](https://www.choicehacking.com/2024/03/21/use-this-framework-for-fascinating-memorable-presentations-backed-by-science/)

### 3. **Surprise**  
A counterintuitive insight that breaks an assumption.  
- Surprise increases memorability and emotional engagement.  
  [Github](https://www.choicehacking.com/2024/03/21/use-this-framework-for-fascinating-memorable-presentations-backed-by-science/)

### 4. **Salient Idea**  
The *one* idea the audience should remember above all else.  
- Not two. Not three. One.  
  [Github](https://www.choicehacking.com/2024/03/21/use-this-framework-for-fascinating-memorable-presentations-backed-by-science/)

### 5. **Story**  
A narrative that is specific enough to be vivid and universal enough to resonate.  
- Humans remember stories far better than abstract statements.  
  [Github](https://www.choicehacking.com/2024/03/21/use-this-framework-for-fascinating-memorable-presentations-backed-by-science/)

## 🧭 Part 3 — The Process (How Winston Structured the Whole Workflow)

### **Phase 1 — Context Intake**  
Before writing anything, gather:
- Topic  
- Audience  
- Occasion  
- Time available  
- Desired outcome  
- Constraints  
- Existing materials  
  [Github](https://github.com/machinarii/awesome-mental-models/blob/master/mental-models/patrick-winstons-mit-communication-framework.md)

### **Phase 2 — Craft the Empowerment Promise**  
One sentence that defines the entire talk.  
  [Github](https://github.com/machinarii/awesome-mental-models/blob/master/mental-models/patrick-winstons-mit-communication-framework.md)

### **Phase 3 — Build Winston’s Star**  
Design the Symbol, Slogan, Surprise, Salient Idea, and Story.  
  [Github](https://github.com/machinarii/awesome-mental-models/blob/master/mental-models/patrick-winstons-mit-communication-framework.md)

### **Phase 4 — Deliver with Tools**  
From his MIT lecture:
- Use boards, props, and simple slides  
- Avoid dense text  
- Never read slides  
- Use whitespace and large fonts  
  [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-tll-005-how-to-speak-january-iap-2018/pages/how-to-speak/)

### **Phase 5 — How to Stop**  
Winston disliked “Thank you” or “Questions?” as final slides.  
Instead, end with:
- A **Contributions Slide** summarizing what the audience gained  
  [Rattibha](https://en.rattibha.com/thread/2039258111543046403)

## 🧠 Why This Framework Works
Winston’s approach is grounded in cognitive psychology:
- People have **one language processor** — they cannot read dense slides and listen simultaneously.   [Medium](https://medium.com/coding-nexus/make-unforgettable-presentations-with-the-secret-mit-framework-8fbef77021ce)  
- Surprise and story increase retention.  
- Symbols and slogans create memory anchors.
